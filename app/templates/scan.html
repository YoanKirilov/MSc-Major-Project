Where to start

What should I check first?

Help me recognise a device
  1. Compare the address with the Wi-Fi or network details on a device you own. You can also ask the person who set up your Wi-Fi to show you the router's connected-device list.
  2. Wake the device if it is asleep. “Try identifying this address again” asks for a reported name; it does not run another full scan or guarantee a name.
  3. Once you recognise it, choose “Add your own nickname”, such as “Living-room TV”. This is your label, not a verified identity.
  4. Read that device's review items. If you do not recognise it or are unsure what to change, ask its owner or maker for help. Do not disable features just to clear a result.
What was checked, and what do priorities mean?

High means review first; Medium means review next; Low means lower priority. Review describes a feature whose safety this tool has not assessed. An answering service is not proof of a break-in; no flagged items is not proof of safety.

        How we explained the results

        AI settings
        Explain the words in this report
        Service or feature
        Something a device offers that other devices may contact, such as file sharing. Answering a check is not automatically a security problem or evidence of use.
        IP address
        A device's address on this network. It can change and does not verify its identity.
        Port, TCP and UDP
        A port is a numbered connection point for a device feature. TCP and UDP are ways devices exchange information. Seeing one does not, by itself, mean a security problem.
        HTTP and HTTPS
        Ways to open a web page. HTTP does not protect information while it travels. HTTPS protects the connection when its certificate is valid; it does not prove the whole device is safe.
        Software fingerprint and CVE
        A fingerprint is a scanner's clue about software and its version, not verified identity. A CVE is a published software problem; a possible match does not prove it affects this device.
        Encryption
        Protection that scrambles information while it travels.
        Evidence strength
        How strongly the recorded facts support an observation, not how safe a device is.
        Device not identified
        No usable name was found. This does not mean an intruder; check your router's list or ask the person who set up the network.
